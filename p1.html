Lesson Proper

What is Data?

Data is a collection of raw facts that have not yet been organized or interpreted. Think of data as individual puzzle pieces. By themselves, they have little meaning, but when combined correctly, they reveal the whole picture.

Examples of Data
  • Student Name: Juan Dela Cruz
  • Age: 20
  • Course: BS Computer Science
  • Quiz Score: 95
  • Purchase Amount: ₱520
Simple Meaning: Data is like individual LEGO blocks. One block cannot build a house. Thousands of blocks together create something meaningful.

What is Information?

Information is processed data that has meaning and can help people make decisions.

Suppose a teacher collects quiz scores from 40 students. The raw scores are data. After computing the average score and identifying the highest and lowest performers, the results become information.

Formula:
Data + Processing = Information

What is a Database?

A database is an organized collection of related data stored electronically so it can be searched, updated, and managed efficiently. Instead of keeping information in notebooks or paper files, organizations store their records inside databases.

Real-Life Example

Imagine your university. Instead of keeping every student's record inside filing cabinets, the school stores information in a Student Information System. The database contains:

  • Student ID
  • Student Name
  • Course
  • Subjects
  • Grades
  • Tuition Balance
Simple Explanation
Think of a database as a digital filing cabinet. Instead of folders made of paper, information is stored inside tables.

Why Are Traditional Databases Not Enough?

Traditional databases are excellent for recording daily transactions. However, they were never designed to analyze years of historical data. Businesses often ask questions like:

  • Which product sold the most last Christmas?
  • Which branch earned the highest revenue?
  • What time do customers usually buy?
  • Which city has the most loyal customers?
  • How much profit did we make in the last five years?

Answering these questions using only an operational database can be slow because millions of records must be searched every time.

Analogy
Imagine searching for one photo inside your phone. Now imagine searching through 10 million photos every time you want to answer one question. That is what large companies experience every day.

What is a Data Warehouse?

A Data Warehouse is a centralized repository that collects data from different systems and stores it in one place for reporting, analytics, and decision-making.

Unlike operational databases that focus on daily transactions, a Data Warehouse is designed to answer business questions quickly.

Simple Meaning
A Data Warehouse is like a huge library. Instead of borrowing books, managers "borrow" information to help them make better business decisions.

Example

A supermarket has different systems.

  • Cashier System
  • Inventory System
  • Payroll System
  • Customer Loyalty System
  • Online Ordering System

Each system stores different information. A Data Warehouse combines all these records into one centralized location. Managers can now create reports without opening five different systems.

How Does a Data Warehouse Work?

Step Description
1 Collect data from different systems.
2 Clean incorrect or duplicate data.
3 Combine all information together.
4 Store the cleaned data inside the warehouse.
5 Create reports, charts and dashboards.

The Four Characteristics of a Data Warehouse

According to Bill Inmon, a Data Warehouse has four important characteristics.

1. Subject-Oriented

Data is organized according to important business subjects instead of daily transactions.

Examples:

  • Students
  • Employees
  • Customers
  • Products
  • Sales
Instead of organizing by departments, we organize by business topics.

2. Integrated

Data comes from many different systems but follows one consistent format.

Example:

  • One system writes "Male"
  • Another writes "M"
  • Another writes "male"

The Data Warehouse converts everything into one standard format.

3. Time-Variant

A Data Warehouse stores historical information.

Instead of keeping only today's sales, it stores many years of data.

Managers can compare: 2022 vs 2023 vs 2024 vs 2025

4. Non-Volatile

Once data has been stored inside the warehouse, it is rarely changed or deleted.

New information is added while old information remains available for historical analysis.

Real-Life Examples of Data Warehousing

🛒 Shopee

Analyzes millions of customer purchases to determine trending products, best-selling brands, and seasonal buying patterns.

🎬 Netflix

Stores viewing history to recommend movies and television shows based on user preferences.

💳 GCash

Analyzes transaction history to detect fraud, spending habits, and financial trends.

🏥 Hospitals

Combine patient records, laboratory results, billing information, and pharmacy transactions into one centralized warehouse.

🎓 Universities

Analyze enrollment trends, student performance, graduation rates, and faculty workload.

☕ Coffee Shops

Identify which drinks sell the most during weekends, holidays, and rainy seasons.
