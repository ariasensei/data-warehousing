⌁ Introduction

Imagine you own a nationwide supermarket. Every day, thousands of products are sold in hundreds of stores. At the end of the year, you may have millions of sales records.

Now your manager asks questions like:

  • Which product sold the most last month?
  • Which region had the highest revenue?
  • Which branch performed best during Christmas?
  • How did sales compare between 2025 and 2026?

Searching through millions of records one by one would take a long time. Instead, businesses use OLAP, which allows users to explore and summarize large amounts of data quickly.

Key Idea //

Think of OLAP as a smart tool that lets managers ask business questions and receive answers in seconds.

What is OLAP?

OLAP (Online Analytical Processing) is a technology used to analyze large amounts of data stored in a Data Warehouse.

Analyze trends
Compare performance
Summarize information
Make business decisions
Simple Definition //

OLAP is a system that allows users to quickly analyze data from different perspectives.

! Why is OLAP Important?

Businesses collect huge amounts of data every day, including sales transactions, student enrollments, hospital admissions, bank transactions, and online purchases.

Business Questions

Managers need quick answers

Which department performs best? What products are most profitable? Which months have the highest sales?

OLAP Contribution

Fast and efficient analysis

OLAP makes answering analytical questions fast, interactive, and useful for decision-making.

⇄ OLTP vs OLAP

Many students confuse OLTP and OLAP. Although they work together, they have different purposes.

OLTP (Online Transaction Processing) OLAP (Online Analytical Processing)
Records daily transactions Analyzes historical data
Supports business operations Supports business decisions
Many small transactions Complex analytical queries
Fast insert, update, delete Fast reporting and analysis
Current data Historical and summarized data

OLTP Example

A customer buys a laptop

The system records the customer, product, payment, and date. This is an operational transaction.

OLAP Example

A manager analyzes laptop sales

How many laptops were sold this year? Which city bought the most? Which month had the highest revenue?

◇ What is an OLAP Cube?

An OLAP Cube is a way of organizing data so that it can be analyzed from multiple perspectives. Although it is called a cube, it does not have to contain only three dimensions. It can have many dimensions depending on business requirements.

Imagine a cube with the dimensions Product, Time, and Location. Each point inside the cube stores a value, such as Sales Amount.

This structure allows users to quickly compare sales by product, month, region, or any combination of dimensions.

PRODUCT LOCATION TIME

Components of an OLAP Cube

Component 01

Facts

Measurable values such as Sales, Profit, Quantity, and Revenue.

Component 02

Dimensions

Categories used for analysis, such as Time, Product, Customer, Branch, and Employee.

Component 03

Measures

Calculations performed on facts, such as SUM(Sales), AVG(Profit), COUNT(Orders), and MAX(Revenue).

⟲ OLAP Operations

One of the strengths of OLAP is that users can explore data in different ways.

OPERATION 01

Roll-Up

Roll-Up summarizes data by moving to a higher level.

Example: Daily Sales → Monthly Sales → Yearly Sales. January ₱100,000 plus February ₱120,000 gives 2026 Total Sales = ₱220,000.
OPERATION 02

Drill-Down

Drill-Down is the opposite of Roll-Up. It shows more detailed information.

Example: Year → Quarter → Month → Day. Managers can start with yearly reports and inspect daily details.
OPERATION 03

Slice

A Slice selects one value from one dimension.

Example: Select Year = 2026. The report now displays only data for 2026.
OPERATION 04

Dice

A Dice selects multiple values from multiple dimensions.

Example: Year = 2026 AND Region = Luzon AND Product Category = Electronics.
OPERATION 05

Pivot (Rotate)

Pivot changes the way data is displayed. The data is the same, but the perspective changes.

Example: Before Pivot: Rows = Products and Columns = Months. After Pivot: Rows = Months and Columns = Products.

▦ Types of OLAP

There are three main types of OLAP systems.

Type 01

MOLAP

Multidimensional OLAP stores data in specialized multidimensional cubes.

Advantages: Very fast analysis and excellent query performance.

Disadvantages: Requires additional storage and cube processing may take time.

Type 02

ROLAP

Relational OLAP keeps data inside relational databases.

Advantages: Handles very large datasets and is easier to update.

Disadvantages: Slower than MOLAP for complex queries.

Type 03

HOLAP

Hybrid OLAP combines MOLAP and ROLAP.

Frequently used summary data is stored in cubes, while detailed data remains in relational databases.

Advantage: Good balance of speed and scalability.

Comparison of OLAP Types

Feature MOLAP ROLAP HOLAP
Storage Multidimensional Cube Relational Database Combination
Speed Very Fast Moderate Fast
Scalability Medium High High

Limitations of OLAP

  • Requires a well-designed Data Warehouse.
  • Initial setup can be expensive.
  • Cube processing may require additional time and storage.
  • Historical data must be accurate and consistent.
  • Very large datasets may require optimized storage strategies.

◉ Real-World Example

An online shopping company stores millions of transactions in its Data Warehouse. Using OLAP, managers can instantly answer questions such as:

  • What were the top five products last quarter?
  • Which city generated the highest revenue?
  • Which payment method is most popular?
  • Which products sell best during holidays?
  • How do this year's sales compare with last year's?

Instead of manually searching millions of records, OLAP performs these analyses within seconds.

Lesson Summary

Online Analytical Processing (OLAP) is a key technology in Data Warehousing and Business Intelligence that enables organizations to analyze large volumes of historical data quickly and efficiently.

Unlike OLTP, which focuses on recording daily transactions, OLAP is designed for reporting, trend analysis, and decision support. Using OLAP cubes, organizations can examine data from multiple dimensions and perform operations such as Roll-Up, Drill-Down, Slice, Dice, and Pivot.

Different OLAP architectures—MOLAP, ROLAP, and HOLAP—provide different approaches to balancing speed, storage, and scalability. Together with the Data Warehouse and ETL processes, OLAP helps businesses transform raw data into meaningful insights that support strategic decision-making.
