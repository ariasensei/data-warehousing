What is a Data Warehouse?

Introduction

A Data Warehouse (DW) is a central repository of integrated data from one or more disparate sources.

  • Stores current and historical data in one single place.
  • Designed specifically to support **Business Intelligence (BI)** and data analytics.
  • Enables decision-makers to run queries, build reports, and derive strategic insights.

Why Do We Need a DW?

Business Context
  • Operational Databases are not built for analytics: Running heavy queries on production databases slows down daily operations.
  • Siloed Data: Organizations store data across separate systems (CRM, ERP, Billing, Web Logs).
  • Historical Trend Analysis: Operational DBs usually hold only current operational state; DW keeps historical trends over time.

OLTP vs. OLAP

Key Concept

OLTP (Online Transaction Processing)

Focus: Day-to-day operations & transactions.

Operations: Fast INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE.

Data: Highly normalized, real-time data.

OLAP (Online Analytical Processing)

Focus: Analysis & reporting for decision-making.

Operations: Complex SELECT / aggregation queries.

Data: Denormalized, historical, integrated data.

4 Core Characteristics of a DW

Bill Inmon Definition
  • Subject-Oriented: Organized around major subjects (e.g., Customers, Sales, Products) rather than applications.
  • Integrated: Data from disparate sources is cleaned, transformed, and unified.
  • Time-Variant: Data is recorded with historical timeframes (e.g., past 5-10 years).
  • Non-Volatile: Data is read-only; historical data is loaded and retained without updates or deletions.

1-Tier Architecture

DW Architectures
  • Concept: Direct link between operational data and reporting interfaces.
  • Goal: Minimize the amount of data stored by removing redundant layers.
  • Drawback: Rarely used in practice because analytical queries directly impact operational system performance.
Data Warehousing Slide 8 of 13

2-Tier Architecture

DW Architectures
  • Separation of Layers: Clear division between the Data Source layer and the Data Warehouse layer.
  • Data is extracted from sources, transformed, and saved into the central warehouse.
  • Clients directly query the Data Warehouse tier.
  • Limitation: Can experience performance bottlenecks with high numbers of concurrent end-users.
Data Warehousing Slide 9 of 13

3-Tier Architecture

DW Architectures
  • Bottom Tier (Database Server): Central DW repository fed via ETL processes.
  • Middle Tier (OLAP Server): Pre-aggregates and organizes data (RROLAP/MOLAP models) for speed.
  • Top Tier (Front-End Client): Query tools, reporting software, and BI dashboards.
Data Warehousing Slide 10 of 13

The ETL Process

Data Integration
  • Extract: Retrieval of data from heterogeneous transactional systems.
  • Transform: Cleaning, filtering, re-formatting, validating, and applying business logic.
  • Load: Writing the transformed data into the target Data Warehouse or Data Mart.

What are Data Marts?

Components
  • A subset of a Data Warehouse focused on a single department or line of business.
  • Examples: Finance Data Mart, Marketing Data Mart, Sales Data Mart.
  • Types:
    • Dependent: Sourced directly from an enterprise DW.
    • Independent: Sourced directly from operational systems (Not recommended).
