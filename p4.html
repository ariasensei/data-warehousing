    Introduction

    Imagine you are preparing fruit juice. You do not simply throw everything into a blender. Instead, you follow three steps.

    Step 01

    Collect the fruits

    Gather apples, oranges, and mangoes.

    Step 02

    Prepare them

    Wash them, remove the seeds, peel the skin, and cut them into pieces.

    Step 03

    Blend and pour

    The juice is ready to drink.

    A Data Warehouse works almost the same way. Business data must first be collected, cleaned, and prepared before it can be stored for reporting. This process is called ETL.

    What is ETL?

    ETL stands for Extract, Transform, and Load. It is the process of collecting data from different sources, cleaning and organizing it, and storing it inside a Data Warehouse.

    Think of ETL as a factory assembly line. Raw materials enter one side, are processed, and come out as finished products. Similarly, raw data enters the ETL process and becomes useful information for business analysis.

    DATA
    SOURCES
    →
    EXTRACT
    →
    TRANSFORM
    →
    LOAD
    →
    DATA WAREHOUSE
    & REPORTS

    Why is ETL Important?

    Businesses collect data from many different systems. A supermarket may have a Sales System, Inventory System, Payroll System, Customer Loyalty System, and Online Store. Each system stores data differently.

    ETL combines all these sources into one Data Warehouse so managers can generate accurate reports.

    Without ETL

    • Data may be incomplete.
    • Reports may contain errors.
    • Departments may have conflicting information.
    • Decision-making becomes unreliable.

    With ETL

    • Data is prepared for analysis.
    • Reports become more accurate.
    • Information is standardized.
    • Decisions become more reliable.

    Step 1: Extract

    Extraction is the process of collecting data from different sources.

    Relational databases
    Excel spreadsheets
    CSV files
    Web applications
    ERP and CRM systems
    APIs and cloud storage

    For example, a company may collect sales from MySQL, inventory from Excel, customers from an online website, and employees from an HR system. All these datasets are extracted.

    The goal of extraction is to collect all required data without changing the original source data. Extraction should be accurate, fast, and reliable.

    Types of Extraction

    Type 01

    Full Extraction

    Copies all available data every time. For example, a company copies every customer record every night.

    Advantages: Simple and complete.
    Disadvantages: Slow and uses more storage.

    Type 02

    Incremental Extraction

    Copies only new or updated records. If 100,000 sales existed yesterday and 500 sales are new today, ETL copies only the 500 new records.

    Advantages: Faster, uses less storage, and is most commonly used.

    Step 2: Transform

    After extraction, the data is rarely ready for analysis. Different systems may store information differently. For example, one system may store Male while another uses M. These values should be standardized before loading.

    The transformation stage improves data quality by cleaning incorrect data, removing duplicates, standardizing formats, validating records, combining datasets, and creating calculated fields.

    1. Data Cleaning

    Correct spelling errors. For example, “Manilla” should become “Manila”.

    2. Remove Duplicates

    Two records for John Santos should become one clean customer record.

    3. Standardize Formats

    Dates such as 01/12/2026 and 2026-12-01 can be standardized as 2026-12-01.

    4. Data Validation

    Check whether values are correct. An age value of -5 is invalid.

    5. Data Integration

    Merge customer, sales, and inventory information from multiple systems.

    6. Derived Fields

    Create new information. Quantity = 5 and Price = ₱400 produces Total Sales = ₱2,000.

    Step 3: Load

    Loading means storing the transformed data inside the Data Warehouse. After loading, Business Intelligence tools such as Power BI or Tableau can analyze the data.

    Full Load

    Replace all warehouse data

    Usually used during the first implementation of a Data Warehouse.

    Incremental Load

    Add only new records

    The most common loading strategy because it is faster and more efficient.

    ETL and Dimensional Modelling

    ETL loads data into Fact Tables and Dimension Tables. The ETL process ensures that each table receives clean and accurate data.

    Sales System
    Extract + Transform
    Fact Sales
    Product, Customer & Date Dimensions

    ETL vs ELT

    You may also hear the term ELT.

    ETL ELT
    Extract → Transform → Load Extract → Load → Transform
    Data is cleaned before entering the warehouse. Data is loaded first, then transformed inside the warehouse.
    Traditional approach. Modern cloud approach.
    Good for smaller systems. Good for large cloud platforms.

    Examples of ELT platforms include Snowflake, Google BigQuery, and Amazon Redshift.

    ETL Workflow Example: Online Shopping System

    Data Sources

    • Website Orders
    • Customer Database
    • Payment Gateway
    • Inventory System

    Workflow Output

    Load cleaned data into Fact Sales, Product Dimension, Customer Dimension, and Date Dimension.

    Managers can analyze daily sales, best-selling products, top customers, and monthly revenue.

    Extract

    Collect all required data.

    Transform

    Remove duplicates, correct invalid records, standardize dates, calculate sales, and match product IDs.

    Load

    Store prepared records in dimensional warehouse tables.

    ETL Design Principles

    Accurate

    No missing or incorrect data.

    Consistent

    The same data should mean the same thing everywhere. “M” and “Male” should be standardized.

    Scalable

    The process should still work as data grows from thousands to millions of records.

    Efficient

    Minimize processing time and storage usage.

    Reliable

    When an error occurs, the ETL process should detect it, log it, and recover without corrupting the warehouse.

    Common Challenges in ETL

    Poor Data Quality

    Missing values, incorrect spelling, and duplicate records.

    Different Data Formats

    CSV, Excel, MySQL, APIs, and cloud databases may all use different structures.

    Large Data Volume

    Millions of records require efficient extraction and loading strategies.

    Slow Performance

    Poorly designed ETL processes can delay reports.

    Security

    Sensitive information, such as customer details, must be protected during extraction, transformation, and loading.

    Best Practices

    ✔ Validate data before loading.
    ✔ Remove duplicate records.
    ✔ Standardize formats.
    ✔ Use incremental loading whenever possible.
    ✔ Log all ETL activities.
    ✔ Monitor ETL performance regularly.
    ✔ Protect sensitive data using encryption and access controls.
    ✔ Test ETL workflows before deployment.

    Real-World Example: Hospital Data Warehouse

    A hospital collects data from Patient Registration, Laboratory, Pharmacy, and Billing. Each department uses its own database.

    The ETL process combines all these records into one Data Warehouse. Hospital administrators can then answer questions such as:

    • How many patients were admitted this month?
    • Which department generated the highest revenue?
    • What are the most common diagnoses?
    • Which medicines are prescribed most often?

    Without ETL, answering these questions would require manually combining data from multiple systems.

    Lesson Summary

    The ETL (Extract, Transform, Load) process is the backbone of every Data Warehouse. It collects data from multiple sources, improves its quality through cleaning and transformation, and stores it in a structured format for analysis.

    A well-designed ETL process ensures that business reports are accurate, consistent, and reliable. Understanding ETL design and implementation prepares students to build data pipelines that support Business Intelligence, analytics, and data-driven decision-making across industries such as retail, healthcare, education, banking, and e-commerce.
