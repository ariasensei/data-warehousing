Meeting 1

Data Warehouse Concepts & Architecture

Foundations of Analytical Data Systems

What Is a Data Warehouse?

Meeting 1 • Part 1

A Data Warehouse is a centralized system used to store and organize large amounts of data from different sources for analysis and decision-making.

"A database is mainly designed to run the business. A data warehouse is mainly designed to analyze the business."

Real-World E-Commerce Example

An online retailer generates daily data across distinct, isolated platforms:

Website orders, Mobile Apps, Payments, Inventory, Shipping, Marketing...

Questions Management Asks: Which product sells most? Which region has highest yield? What is the multi-year customer growth trajectory?

Why Do We Need a Data Warehouse?

Meeting 1 • Part 2

Without a centralized warehouse, data remains isolated across distinct transactional silos. Centralization enables unified Business Intelligence (BI).

OLTP vs. OLAP

Meeting 1 • Part 4

OLTP (Online Transaction Processing)

  • Used for everyday operational tasks.
  • Examples: Buying a product, booking tickets, registering students, updating addresses.
  • Key Focus: High concurrency, fast execution of simple operations.
OLTP = RUN THE BUSINESS

OLAP (Online Analytical Processing)

  • Used for complex analytical insights.
  • Examples: Monthly sales analysis, revenue forecasting, cohort retention tracking.
  • Key Focus: Processing massive volumes for insights.
OLAP = STUDY THE BUSINESS

ETL vs. ELT Data Ingestion

Meeting 1 • Parts 6 & 7

ETL (Extract ➔ Transform ➔ Load)

SOURCE ──► EXTRACT ──► TRANSFORM ──► LOAD ──► WAREHOUSE

Data is modified on an external staging server before loading into target storage. Traditional enterprise standard.

ELT (Extract ➔ Load ➔ Transform)

SOURCE ──► EXTRACT ──► LOAD ──► PLATFORM ──► TRANSFORM

Raw data is dumped directly into modern cloud engines (Lakehouse/Databricks), transforming natively using cloud compute.

ETL vs ELT Comparison

Meeting 1 • Part 8
Property ETL ELT
Transformation OrderTransform before loadingLoad before transforming
Platform AlignmentTraditional Data WarehouseModern Cloud Lakehouses / Storage
Processing LocationExternal Staging EngineTarget System Native Compute Engine
FlexibilityRigid schema definitions requiredRaw retention allows on-demand transformation
"Neither pattern is inherently superior. Choice depends on target compute power, network infrastructure, security regulations, and data structures."

Fact vs Dimension: Easy Rule

Meeting 1 • Part 11

FACT

What happened?

Stores measurable numerical quantities, transactions, and FK references.

DIMENSION

Describes what happened.

Provides context (Who, What, Where, When, Why, How).

Real-world breakdown: "A customer purchased 3 laptops for ₱150,000 in Manila."
• Facts (Metrics): Quantity = 3, Sales = ₱150,000
• Dimensions (Context): Customer = Maria, Product = Laptop, Location = Manila

Why Use a Star Schema?

  • Simplified analytics structure: Denormalized design minimizes complex SQL join paths.
  • Enables efficient multi-dimensional aggregation queries across business domains.

Snowflake Schema & Comparison

Meeting 1 • Parts 14 & 15

In a Snowflake Schema, dimension tables are normalized into multiple sub-tables (e.g., Product ➔ Category ➔ Department).

Star SchemaSnowflake Schema
Denormalized dimension tablesNormalized dimension structures
Fewer joins required (Faster read performance)More complex JOIN relationships
Higher storage redundancyReduces data redundancy / lower disk usage
Ideal for general analytical queriesIdeal for specific strict structural hierarchies

Meeting 2

Data Quality, Cleaning, and Governance

Trustworthy Analytics and Enterprise Standards

The Most Important Question

Meeting 2 • Part 1
"What happens if the underlying enterprise data is wrong?"

If financial reports state Sales = ₱10 Million, but actual real revenues were ₱8 Million, executive management executes strategy based on erroneous indicators.

  • Flawed budget allocations & incorrect financial projections
  • Faulty automated operational strategies
  • Inaccurate business intelligence metrics
GIGO: Garbage In, Garbage Out. Bad data guarantees failed Machine Learning models.

What Is Data Quality?

Meeting 2 • Part 2

Data Quality measures how suitable and reliable a dataset is for its intended analytical purpose.

A dataset with 100,000,000 rows does not guarantee value if it contains missing, distorted, or corrupted entries.

Is it Accurate?

Is it Complete?

Is it Consistent?

Is it Valid?

Is it Unique?

Is it Timely?

Dimensions of Data Quality (1 - 3)

Meeting 2 • Part 3

1. Accuracy

Does data reflect real-world values?

Error: Storing student age as 91 instead of 21.

2. Completeness

Are mandatory values present?

Error: Missing Age = NULL fields in demographic models.

3. Consistency

Are values formatted uniformly across sources?

Error: Mixing PH, PHL, Phil., and Philippines.

Dimensions of Data Quality (4 - 6)

Meeting 2 • Part 3

4. Validity

Does data obey specified domain rules?

Error: Negative ages like Age = -5 or invalid dates.

5. Uniqueness

Are duplicate entities present?

Error: Duplicate transaction IDs inflating revenue metrics.

6. Timeliness

Is the data current enough for actionable use?

Error: Using 5-year-old weather data to predict today's logistics.

Profiling & Handling Missing Data

Meeting 2 • Parts 4 & 5

Data Profiling: Inspecting structural properties, missingness, distributions, and value frequencies before performing analytical operations.

Handling Missing (NULL) Data:

Option A: Record Removal

Drop rows when missingness percentage is negligible and non-systemic.

Option B: Imputation

Replace null values with statistical metrics (Mean, Median, Mode) or domain heuristics.

Warning: Never blindly replace missing entries without understanding the underlying root cause.

Duplicates & Cleaning Operations

Meeting 2 • Parts 6 & 7

Systemic duplicate records artificially inflate aggregate analytical metrics:

Actual Sales: ₱10,000  |  With Duplicates: ₱30,000 (Severe Error)

Core Data Cleaning Operations

  • Deduplicating unique entity records
  • Handling missing field values
  • Standardizing string / date formats
  • Enforcing schema type casting
  • Correcting domain constraint violations
  • Removing noisy / irrelevant fields

Data Governance Frameworks

Meeting 2 • Parts 10 & 11

Data Governance is the holistic framework of rules, roles, standards, and controls applied to manage data assets securely and effectively.

Core Questions Governance Answers

  • Who has authorized access to sensitive student records?
  • Who is directly accountable for correcting flawed entries?
  • What standardization formats govern dates and money?
  • What is the mandatory legal retention policy for log data?
Without governance, data management degrades into fragmented, unorganized silos with significant security, compliance, and operational risks.

Components of Data Governance

Meeting 2 • Part 12

Data Ownership

Individuals accountable for specific domain datasets.

Data Stewardship

Tactical roles managing data quality rules and pipelines.

Data Security

Access control rules, role-based controls, and encryption policies.

Data Privacy

Compliance with regulations governing sensitive information.

Data Standards

Shared naming formats, schema types, and metric definitions.

Data Lifecycle

Policies for long-term archiving, retention, and deletion.

Metadata: Data About Data

Meeting 2 • Parts 13 & 14

Metadata provides context, structure, and definition for stored raw data.

Example Field: Revenue

Without metadata, users don't know if values represent gross revenue, net revenue, or local currency metrics.

Metadata Context: "Total net sales value of completed customer transactions, measured in PHP (₱), excluding local VAT."

Why Data Scientists Need Metadata

Merging two datasets containing an income column can corrupt ML models if Dataset A stores monthly income while Dataset B stores annual income.

Machine Learning Impact

Meeting 3

Performance Optimization

Scalable Data Processing & Efficiency

What Is Performance Optimization?

Meeting 3 • Parts 1 & 2

Performance Optimization improves data processing efficiency, reducing runtime, resource usage, and platform cost.

Small Scale (1 Million Rows)

Execution runtime: ~2 seconds.

Acceptable performance level.

Big Data Scale (500 Million Rows)

Execution runtime: 10+ minutes.

Requires query optimization.

Data scientists work with social media streams, financial ledgers, and sensor logs that easily scale into billions of rows.

Query Performance: Avoid SELECT *

Meeting 3 • Parts 3 & 4

Requesting all columns across massive tables forces expensive disk I/O and network overhead.

Inefficient Query

SELECT * 
FROM sales;

Scans all 50 columns across 500M rows unnecessarily.

Optimized Query

SELECT 
    product_id, 
    quantity, 
    sales_amount 
FROM sales;

Reads only the 3 specific columns required.

Filtering & Aggregation Strategies

Meeting 3 • Parts 5 & 6

Early Filtering (WHERE)

SELECT product_id, sales_amount
FROM sales
WHERE order_date >= '2026-01-01';

Reduces the volume of data processed early in the query lifecycle.

Aggregation Pushdown

SELECT 
    Month, 
    SUM(sales_amount) 
FROM sales 
GROUP BY Month;

Summarizes raw records into compact metric rollups.

Optimizing Table Joins

Meeting 3 • Part 7

Joining un-indexed or unstructured large tables causes massive computational and memory strain.

SELECT 
    p.category, 
    SUM(f.sales_amount) AS total_sales
FROM fact_sales f
JOIN dim_product p ON f.product_id = p.product_id
GROUP BY p.category;
Join Guideline: Filter tables before executing join operations, and ensure join keys are appropriately indexed or aligned in storage.

Query Execution Plans & EXPLAIN

Meeting 3 • Parts 8 & 9 
SQL Query ──► Query Planner ──► Execution Plan ──► Engine Execution ──► Output Result

Database engines evaluate multiple physical strategies to run a query. Use EXPLAIN to view the underlying plan.

EXPLAIN 
SELECT category, SUM(sales_amount) 
FROM sales 
GROUP BY category;
Analyze plans to spot full table scans, expensive shuffles, and costly join operations.

Indexing Data Structures

Meeting 3 • Parts 10 & 11

An Index is a data structure that speeds up row lookups, similar to an index in a textbook.

When Indexes Help

  • Columns used frequently in WHERE filters
  • Foreign Key columns evaluated in JOIN conditions
  • Primary lookup fields (e.g., customer_id)

Trade-offs & Costs

  • Consumes additional disk storage space
  • Adds overhead during write operations (INSERT/UPDATE)
  • Requires ongoing index maintenance

Partitioning Concepts

Meeting 3 • Parts 12 & 13

Partitioning physically divides large tables into distinct directories or segments based on column values (e.g., Year).

Sales Table Storage
├── Year=2024 (30 Million Rows)
├── Year=2025 (30 Million Rows)
└── Year=2026 (40 Million Rows)
Partition Pruning: When querying 2026 sales, the engine skips the 2024 and 2025 directories entirely, scanning only 40M rows instead of 100M rows.

Over-Partitioning & Aggregations

Meeting 3 • Parts 14 & 15

Partitioning Warning

Creating millions of tiny partitions causes significant metadata management overhead and degrades performance.

Strategy: Choose partition keys with appropriate cardinality (e.g., Date vs. User ID).

Pre-Aggregation Optimizations

RAW TRANSACTIONS
      ↓
DAILY SUMMARIES
      ↓
MONTHLY ROLLUPS

Pre-calculating rollups prevents re-computing metrics over billions of raw rows repeatedly.

ETL Tuning & Incremental Processing

Meeting 3 • Parts 16 & 17

ETL pipelines running for hours delay business analytics and insights.

Full Batch Load (Inefficient)

Re-processing all 1 Billion historical records every night to add 10,000 new transactions.

Incremental Load (Optimized)

Processing only the 10,000 new or modified records introduced today, leaving historical data intact.

Databricks, Costs, & Strategy

Meeting 3 • Parts 18 - 20

Databricks & Delta

Leverage commands like OPTIMIZE and Z-ORDER to compact files and index multi-dimensional data.

Performance vs. Cost

Balance compute resource expenses against required runtime SLA targets.

Optimization Strategy

  1. Measure baseline
  2. Identify bottlenecks
  3. Change ONE variable
  4. Measure & evaluate
